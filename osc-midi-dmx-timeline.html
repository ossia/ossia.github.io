An OSC, MIDI and DMX timeline

ossia score can be used to write automations for any OSC, MIDI or DMX parameter on a timeline, which can also wait for external events.

Free and open-source (GPLv3) · Windows, macOS, Linux, Raspberry Pi and web browser

ossia score started as a sequencer for OSC: every parameter of every device on the network is an address, which can be automated, recorded, mapped and placed in time. It is used to drive patches, game engines, media servers, lights or robots, and the same timeline can also contain sound, video, 3D and shaders.

Automations

An automation curve can target an OSC address, a MIDI CC, a DMX channel or a parameter of one of score’s own processes (writing automations); when the score has to follow a beat, tempo, time signatures and quantization can be enabled (musical features).

The timeline does not have to be linear: a trigger waits for an incoming message, a condition chooses between branches, and a loop repeats until something happens (breaking the timeline).

Devices and protocols

Software which supports OSCQuery, for instance patches and applications using libossia, appears in the device explorer with all its parameters; dragging a parameter into the score fills in its address. Plain OSC devices can be discovered on the local network through Bonjour/Zeroconf, and the parameters of a score can be published the same way.

OSC is supported over UDP, TCP (as client or server), WebSockets and serial ports, with SLIP and COBS framing, and host names can be used instead of IP addresses. The same project can also use MIDI, DMX (Art-Net, sACN, DMX USB), MQTT, CoAP, HTTP, LSL and CAN.

The inputs include gamepads, Wiimotes, Leap Motion, MIDI controllers, Arduino over serial, Bluetooth LE sensors, depth cameras, and tracking protocols such as PosiStageNet, TUIO, RTTrP and OpenTrackIO (sensors to OSC). For MIDI, score supports virtual ports, the network MIDI ports provided by the operating system, MIDI file playback, filters, Scala microtonal scales, and the computer keyboard as a MIDI input (MIDI).

Mapping and filtering

The mapper and the value filters are placed between an input and its target: calibration, deadzones, noise gate, hysteresis, debounce and easing curves are available, which is in particular useful for sensors (value filter, calibrator, mapper). Mappings can be written as curves, in JavaScript or as math expressions, and OSC address patterns send a value to a whole group of devices at once (pattern matching).

Any device can be recorded to CSV and replayed later, including incoming DMX (recording); the browser remote gives a simple control surface on a phone, with nothing to install.

score has the same features on Windows, macOS, Linux and the Raspberry Pi, except those which depend on a given system or piece of hardware (Spout, Syphon, CUDA, etc.).

With your own software

score supports OSCQuery natively through libossia, which also exists for Max/MSP, Pure Data, Unity, Python, SuperCollider, openFrameworks and Qt: adding a few objects to a patch or a project is enough for its parameters to appear in score.

More ways to use ossia score

Stage and music

  • Show control
    Cues, scenes and timecode for theater, concerts and events.
  • Live electronic music
    Synths, drum machines, sampler, step sequencers and plug-ins, in sync with MIDI clock, timecode and visuals.
  • Spatial audio
    DBAP, GBAP and trajectories over any loudspeaker layout, up to 1024 channels, and control of spatialization renderers.
  • Dance and interactive theater
    Pose detection, depth cameras, sensors and tracking drive sound, video and light, inside cues and scenes.
  • Contemporary music and live electronics
    Interactive scores that wait for the performers, cues from pedals, live processing and spatial sound.

Video and light

  • Video, mapping and VJing
    Video playback, multi-projector mapping, shaders, 3D and NDI, synchronized with sound and light.
  • DMX lighting and LEDs
    Art-Net, sACN and DMX USB with a fixture library, LED pixel mapping, all in sync with sound and video.
  • Generative art
    Shaders, 3D, gaussian splats and live coding, performed live or rendered frame by frame.
  • Recording and streaming
    Cameras, SDI, NDI and SRT in; ProRes or MP4 recording, RTMP streaming and NDI or Spout out.

Spaces and experiences

  • Interactive installations
    Sensors, cameras and tracking, sound and video, running unattended on a PC or a Raspberry Pi.
  • Museums and exhibitions
    Exhibits that run all day without staff, with monitoring, a staff control panel and kiosk apps.
  • Escape rooms and attractions
    Puzzles, props and sensors driving sound, video and lights, with game logic and a game master panel.
  • Kinetic art and robotics
    Motors, pumps and robots written on a timeline, reacting to sensors, in sync with sound, video and lights.

Tools and research

  • Visual programming
    Patch audio, video and interaction with nodes and a timeline, and run your Pure Data patches.
  • Real-time AI
    Pose detection, depth from a webcam, real-time image generation, language models and machine learning by example, run locally.
  • Protocol bridge
    Convert between MIDI, OSC, DMX, MQTT, serial, HTTP and Bluetooth LE, with mapping and filtering, always on.
  • Research, sonification and biosignals
    LSL, HDF5 and CSV data, sonification, biofeedback, and sessions recorded and replayed on a timeline.
  • Teaching and workshops
    One free tool for sound, video, light, sensors and code, on every OS, in the browser and on Raspberry Pi.
  • Custom apps
    Package a project with your own interface as an app for Windows, macOS, Linux or the web.
