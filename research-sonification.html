Research, sonification and biosignals

ossia score can be used to play data from lab hardware and datasets as sound, light or images, record and replay sessions, and write experiments on a timeline.

Free and open-source (GPLv3) · Windows, macOS, Linux, Raspberry Pi and web browser

ossia score comes from research done at LaBRI and SCRIME (project history), and can be used to connect scientific hardware and datasets to sound, video and light, for experiments, for sonification, or for pieces made with physiological data. For instance, Erin Gee’s BioSynth light show (2025) drives LEDs and DMX lights from biosignals, with score running headless on a Raspberry Pi, and in VIB.E-MOTION (Fheel Concepts and Corinne Linder, 2024), a participatory circus performance is driven by the heart rates of the audience.

BioSynth light show, Erin Gee: an LED and DMX light show driven by biosignals, run by ossia score headless on a Raspberry Pi.

Data sources

Lab Streaming Layer (LSL) gives access to EEG and other physiological devices, and score also supports the CAN bus with DBC databases, serial ports, Bluetooth LE sensors, and any device which speaks OSC or MQTT (LSL, CAN, serial). HDF5 and CSV files are opened directly, including large columns, and played as curves, arrays or textures (HDF5, data processing).

Sonification and visualization

The data can be mapped to synthesis, spatialized sound, shaders, 3D or DMX lights, and plotted in real time (data visualization, Teleplot, signal display). For biofeedback, the heart rate and other physiological signals drive sound, video and light after smoothing, filtering and statistics (accumulator, value filter).

Sessions and protocols

Any device can be recorded to CSV and replayed later, with the audio recorded alongside (recording, CSV recorder), so that a session can be reproduced exactly. The experimental protocol itself can be written as a timeline with conditions, and automated through the JavaScript API (scripting API).

Custom C++ objects can be added, libossia exposes the parameters of other software, and custom render pipelines can be used for graphics research (plug-ins); score itself is free and open-source (GPLv3), and its project files open on Windows, macOS and Linux, which makes it possible for other labs to run the same setup.

More ways to use ossia score

Stage and music

  • Show control
    Cues, scenes and timecode for theater, concerts and events.
  • Live electronic music
    Synths, drum machines, sampler, step sequencers and plug-ins, in sync with MIDI clock, timecode and visuals.
  • Spatial audio
    DBAP, GBAP and trajectories over any loudspeaker layout, up to 1024 channels, and control of spatialization renderers.
  • Dance and interactive theater
    Pose detection, depth cameras, sensors and tracking drive sound, video and light, inside cues and scenes.
  • Contemporary music and live electronics
    Interactive scores that wait for the performers, cues from pedals, live processing and spatial sound.

Video and light

  • Video, mapping and VJing
    Video playback, multi-projector mapping, shaders, 3D and NDI, synchronized with sound and light.
  • DMX lighting and LEDs
    Art-Net, sACN and DMX USB with a fixture library, LED pixel mapping, all in sync with sound and video.
  • Generative art
    Shaders, 3D, gaussian splats and live coding, performed live or rendered frame by frame.
  • Recording and streaming
    Cameras, SDI, NDI and SRT in; ProRes or MP4 recording, RTMP streaming and NDI or Spout out.

Spaces and experiences

  • Interactive installations
    Sensors, cameras and tracking, sound and video, running unattended on a PC or a Raspberry Pi.
  • Museums and exhibitions
    Exhibits that run all day without staff, with monitoring, a staff control panel and kiosk apps.
  • Escape rooms and attractions
    Puzzles, props and sensors driving sound, video and lights, with game logic and a game master panel.
  • Kinetic art and robotics
    Motors, pumps and robots written on a timeline, reacting to sensors, in sync with sound, video and lights.

Tools and research

  • OSC, MIDI and DMX timeline
    Automate any OSC, MIDI or DMX parameter on an interactive timeline, with triggers and conditions.
  • Visual programming
    Patch audio, video and interaction with nodes and a timeline, and run your Pure Data patches.
  • Real-time AI
    Pose detection, depth from a webcam, real-time image generation, language models and machine learning by example, run locally.
  • Protocol bridge
    Convert between MIDI, OSC, DMX, MQTT, serial, HTTP and Bluetooth LE, with mapping and filtering, always on.
  • Teaching and workshops
    One free tool for sound, video, light, sensors and code, on every OS, in the browser and on Raspberry Pi.
  • Custom apps
    Package a project with your own interface as an app for Windows, macOS, Linux or the web.
