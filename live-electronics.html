Contemporary music and live electronics

ossia score can be used to write the electronic part of a piece for instruments, with cues from a pedal, live processing, spatialization, and a score that waits for the musicians.

ossia score comes from the research on interactive scores for music started at LaBRI and SCRIME in Bordeaux with the i-score project (project history); it was designed for pieces where musicians and electronics play together, the timing being written while the performers decide when each section starts. For instance, in Pía Baltazar’s The Ripples of Consequence (Le Vivier, Montréal, 2025), a composition for the Table de Babel performed by Audréanne Filion, the electronics are run by score.

Interactive score

A trigger holds the electronics until something happens on stage, for instance a pedal, a MIDI note, an OSC message, or the audio input crossing a threshold, and conditions choose between branches (breaking the timeline). The cues are given from MIDI pedals, keyboards and controllers, or from a key of the computer keyboard (MIDI input, cues); when the piece has a pulse, tempo, time signatures and quantization can be used next to the free sections driven by the performers (musical features).

Processing the instruments

Live processing uses Faust, VST, VST3, CLAP, LV2 and JSFX effects or the effects built into score, and the instruments can be recorded into the score during the performance (Faust, audio plug-ins, audio recorder). Envelope following, beat tracking and audio descriptors analyze the instruments and drive the processing, sound files, video and lights (envelope follower, analysis).

Existing Pure Data patches run inside the score, or are controlled from it over OSC with libossia (Pure Data); the spatialization in the hall (DBAP, GBAP, trajectories, external renderers) is described on the spatial audio page.

Rehearsals and later performances

During rehearsals, Play from here restarts from any point of the piece. The whole electronic part, with its cues, processing and sound files, is a single project file which opens on every operating system, so that it can be performed again later by other musicians.

For music played with synthesizers, sequencers and loops, see the live electronic music page; other concerts made with score, such as Alain Bonardi’s pianotronics #3, are presented in the gallery.

More ways to use ossia score

Stage and music

  • Show control
    Cues, scenes and timecode for theater, concerts and events.
  • Live electronic music
    Synths, drum machines, sampler, step sequencers and plug-ins, in sync with MIDI clock, timecode and visuals.
  • Spatial audio
    DBAP, GBAP and trajectories over any loudspeaker layout, up to 1024 channels, and control of spatialization renderers.
  • Dance and interactive theater
    Pose detection, depth cameras, sensors and tracking drive sound, video and light, inside cues and scenes.

Video and light

  • Video, mapping and VJing
    Video playback, multi-projector mapping, shaders, 3D and NDI, synchronized with sound and light.
  • DMX lighting and LEDs
    Art-Net, sACN and DMX USB with a fixture library, LED pixel mapping, all in sync with sound and video.
  • Generative art
    Shaders, 3D, gaussian splats and live coding, performed live or rendered frame by frame.
  • Recording and streaming
    Cameras, SDI, NDI and SRT in; ProRes or MP4 recording, RTMP streaming and NDI or Spout out.

Spaces and experiences

  • Interactive installations
    Sensors, cameras and tracking, sound and video, running unattended on a PC or a Raspberry Pi.
  • Museums and exhibitions
    Exhibits that run all day without staff, with monitoring, a staff control panel and kiosk apps.
  • Escape rooms and attractions
    Puzzles, props and sensors driving sound, video and lights, with game logic and a game master panel.
  • Kinetic art and robotics
    Motors, pumps and robots written on a timeline, reacting to sensors, in sync with sound, video and lights.

Tools and research

  • OSC, MIDI and DMX timeline
    Automate any OSC, MIDI or DMX parameter on an interactive timeline, with triggers and conditions.
  • Visual programming
    Patch audio, video and interaction with nodes and a timeline, and run your Pure Data patches.
  • Real-time AI
    Pose detection, depth from a webcam, real-time image generation, language models and machine learning by example, run locally.
  • Protocol bridge
    Convert between MIDI, OSC, DMX, MQTT, serial, HTTP and Bluetooth LE, with mapping and filtering, always on.
  • Research, sonification and biosignals
    LSL, HDF5 and CSV data, sonification, biofeedback, and sessions recorded and replayed on a timeline.
  • Teaching and workshops
    One free tool for sound, video, light, sensors and code, on every OS, in the browser and on Raspberry Pi.
  • Custom apps
    Package a project with your own interface as an app for Windows, macOS, Linux or the web.
