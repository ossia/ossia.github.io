Show control

ossia score can be used to write the cue sheet of a show on a timeline, where some parts wait for the stage manager or for something happening on stage.

ossia score can be used to run a show: sound, video, lights and OSC or MIDI devices are placed on a timeline, and some parts of this timeline wait for an external event, for instance a GO from the stage manager, a key on the keyboard, a MIDI note, an OSC message sent by another computer or a sensor on stage. The software comes from the research on interactive scores done at LaBRI and SCRIME in Bordeaux; a show in score is thus a score where some parts are fixed in time and others are triggered during the performance. For instance, the hip-hop dance show Walking Down The Street (Lina Rahal and Jean-Marie Colin, 2021) is entirely driven by score and controlled live with a Karlax.

Cues and scenes

A cue stores the state of the OSC, MIDI and DMX devices; it is recalled from the timeline, by hand, or from a MIDI controller (cues). Since version 3.9, the parameters of the processes inside score (the dry/wet of a reverb, the speed of a shader, etc.) can be cued and automated the same way as those of an external device.

The show is split into scenes which each wait for their GO; during rehearsals, Play from here restarts from any point (scenes). The exclusive mode of a scenario only lets one interval play at a time, which is useful in particular for sounds or videos that replace each other.

Media and devices

A show can contain:

  • multichannel sound files with time-stretching, HAP and H.264 video, VST, VST3, CLAP and LV2 plug-ins (sound files, video);
  • DMX lights over Art-Net, sACN or a DMX USB interface, with a fixture library (DMX);
  • any device that speaks OSC, OSCQuery, MIDI, HTTP, WebSockets, MQTT or goes through a serial port;
  • the projectors, video switchers and cameras supported by the Bitfocus Companion modules, installed from the package manager (Companion);
  • the launch of other programs or scripts (process launcher).

The project tools gather the media in the project folder, report missing files and make a zip archive of the show, which is handy when touring.

Timecode

score reads and writes LTC, MIDI clock and MIDI timecode (LTC). When following an incoming timecode, the synchronizer changes the playback speed progressively instead of jumping from one position to another (timecode synchronizer); a scenario can also be scrubbed with the mouse, or played backwards.

During the show

The show can be started and stopped from a web browser, for instance on a phone, or from any OSC controller (remote control). MCU-compatible control surfaces follow the selected process, and a MIDI controller described once shows up with the name of each of its knobs and faders (MIDI controllers).

score runs on Windows, macOS, Linux and the Raspberry Pi with the same features, except those which depend on a given system or piece of hardware (Spout, Syphon, CUDA, etc.). It can run a show on its own, or drive over OSC, MIDI or DMX what is already in place: media server, lighting desk, DAW.

More shows are presented in the gallery; the documentation details each of these features.

More ways to use ossia score

Stage and music

  • Live electronic music
    Synths, drum machines, sampler, step sequencers and plug-ins, in sync with MIDI clock, timecode and visuals.
  • Spatial audio
    DBAP, GBAP and trajectories over any loudspeaker layout, up to 1024 channels, and control of spatialization renderers.
  • Dance and interactive theater
    Pose detection, depth cameras, sensors and tracking drive sound, video and light, inside cues and scenes.
  • Contemporary music and live electronics
    Interactive scores that wait for the performers, cues from pedals, live processing and spatial sound.

Video and light

  • Video, mapping and VJing
    Video playback, multi-projector mapping, shaders, 3D and NDI, synchronized with sound and light.
  • DMX lighting and LEDs
    Art-Net, sACN and DMX USB with a fixture library, LED pixel mapping, all in sync with sound and video.
  • Generative art
    Shaders, 3D, gaussian splats and live coding, performed live or rendered frame by frame.
  • Recording and streaming
    Cameras, SDI, NDI and SRT in; ProRes or MP4 recording, RTMP streaming and NDI or Spout out.

Spaces and experiences

  • Interactive installations
    Sensors, cameras and tracking, sound and video, running unattended on a PC or a Raspberry Pi.
  • Museums and exhibitions
    Exhibits that run all day without staff, with monitoring, a staff control panel and kiosk apps.
  • Escape rooms and attractions
    Puzzles, props and sensors driving sound, video and lights, with game logic and a game master panel.
  • Kinetic art and robotics
    Motors, pumps and robots written on a timeline, reacting to sensors, in sync with sound, video and lights.

Tools and research

  • OSC, MIDI and DMX timeline
    Automate any OSC, MIDI or DMX parameter on an interactive timeline, with triggers and conditions.
  • Visual programming
    Patch audio, video and interaction with nodes and a timeline, and run your Pure Data patches.
  • Real-time AI
    Pose detection, depth from a webcam, real-time image generation, language models and machine learning by example, run locally.
  • Protocol bridge
    Convert between MIDI, OSC, DMX, MQTT, serial, HTTP and Bluetooth LE, with mapping and filtering, always on.
  • Research, sonification and biosignals
    LSL, HDF5 and CSV data, sonification, biofeedback, and sessions recorded and replayed on a timeline.
  • Teaching and workshops
    One free tool for sound, video, light, sensors and code, on every OS, in the browser and on Raspberry Pi.
  • Custom apps
    Package a project with your own interface as an app for Windows, macOS, Linux or the web.
